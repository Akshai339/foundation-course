# The Product Design Foundation
### A 12-week course built for a visual/motion designer who already knows how to make things beautiful

---

## Why this course is not like the others

Most UX courses are written for people who have never designed anything. They spend three weeks on colour theory, two on typography, and then hand you a Figma file and call you a product designer.

You don't need that. You have spent years on visual craft, illustration, and motion. You have run marketing campaigns and GTM, which means you already understand *positioning, audience, and why anyone should care* — a thing most junior product designers are catastrophically bad at.

**What you're actually missing is the "product" in product design.** Specifically:

| You already have | You are missing |
|---|---|
| Visual hierarchy, composition, colour, type | The cognitive science of *why* hierarchy works, so you can defend it |
| Motion craft, timing, easing | Motion as *feedback and orientation*, not decoration |
| Audience empathy from marketing | Structured needfinding that survives contact with engineering |
| The ability to make a screen | The ability to decide *which screen should exist* |
| Taste | Rationale — being able to say why, under pressure, to a skeptical PM |
| Speed with AI tools | Judgment about which of the 40 AI-generated options is right |

That last row is the whole thesis. You said it yourself: AI has made *producing* UI easy. That is exactly why the differentiated skill has moved. The bottleneck is no longer execution. It is **problem framing, structural thinking, and defensible judgment.** This course attacks that bottleneck directly and refuses to waste your time on things you can already do.

## The shape of it

**12 weeks · 6 days a week · 90–120 minutes a day · ~130 hours**

Day 7 is deliberately empty. It is for catching up, going deeper on something that hooked you, or resting. Do not schedule it. A course you fall behind on by design is a course you quit.

### Four phases

```
PHASE 1  ·  Weeks 1–3   ·  FOUNDATIONS      Why things work
PHASE 2  ·  Weeks 4–6   ·  THE PRODUCT LAYER Deciding what to build
PHASE 3  ·  Weeks 7–9   ·  THE CRAFT LAYER   Making it real
PHASE 4  ·  Weeks 10–12 ·  THE PRO LAYER     Shipping, and being trusted
```

### Three studios running underneath

The daily work feeds three real projects. You do not "do exercises." You build three things, and the lessons are the tools you pick up along the way.

- **Studio A — The Teardown & Rebuild** (Weeks 1–4)
- **Studio B — Zero to One** (Weeks 5–8)
- **Studio C — The AI Feature** (Weeks 9–12) ← your capstone and portfolio piece

Full briefs in [[03 The Studios]].

## The daily ritual

Every day has exactly three parts. Do them in order. The order matters — the concept primes you to read the source critically, and the source gives you ammunition for the drill.

| | | Time |
|---|---|---|
| **1. CONCEPT** | The lesson. Written for you, in this vault. Read it slowly, once. | 20–25 min |
| **2. SOURCE** | One link to the real thing — a paper, a chapter, a talk. Go to the primary source. | 25–35 min |
| **3. DRILL** | Make something. Always concrete. Always produces an artifact. | 45–60 min |

**The rule that makes this work:** never skip the drill in favour of more reading. Reading feels like progress and isn't. The drill is where the base actually forms.

1. **Critique your actual output** against the specific principles you were supposed to apply that day.
2. **Catch you when you're being a visual designer.** Your failure mode will be making it look good before it works. I'll flag it.

## Checkpoints

At the end of each phase there's an assessment — a **cold transfer test**, not a quiz. You apply that phase's tools to something you haven't seen, under time pressure, then score yourself on five axes and compare against your own baseline.

| | After | Time | |
|---|---|---|---|
| **01** The Cold Diagnosis | Week 03 | 90 min | Can you diagnose an interface on demand, without familiarity? |
| **02** The Framing Test | Week 06 | 2 hrs | Can you go from a vague request to a structural spec — and tell what you know from what you're assuming? |
| **03** The Handoff | Week 09 | 2 hrs | Does your system work without you in the room? |
| **04** The Defence | Week 12 | 2 hrs | Does the work survive other people — and do you? |

Full briefs in [[05 Checkpoints]]. The app surfaces each one automatically when its phase closes.

**The number that matters** isn't the total — it's whether the *shape* flattens. Your Week 1 profile is Craft 5 and everything else 1–2. If Craft is still the outlier at Checkpoint 03, you've been retreating into what you were already good at.

## One promise, one warning

**The promise:** at the end of 12 weeks you will not be a "visual designer who also does UI." You will have a defensible model of how humans process interfaces, a repeatable process for going from fuzzy problem to shipped flow, and three pieces of work that prove it.

**The warning:** the first three weeks will feel slow and theoretical, and you will be tempted to skip to the pretty parts. The theory *is* the base you said you're missing. Everything after Week 4 depends on it. Sit through it.

Start with [[Week 01 — The Discipline and the Machine in the Head]].
