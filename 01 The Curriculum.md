# The Curriculum — all 72 days

Scan-ahead map. Each day is CONCEPT → SOURCE → DRILL, 90–120 minutes. Week 7 in each block is deliberately blank.

← back to [[00 Start Here]]

---

## PHASE 1 · FOUNDATIONS — *why things work*
> The goal of this phase is to replace "it feels right" with "it works because." By the end you will have a mechanical model of a human using an interface.

### [[Week 01 — The Discipline and the Machine in the Head]]
1. What product design actually is — and the four things it isn't
2. Norman's Action Cycle and the two Gulfs
3. Affordances, signifiers, constraints, mappings
4. Conceptual models vs mental models — your real product
5. Direct manipulation and why it feels good
6. **Studio A kickoff** — pick your target, first-pass diagnosis

### [[Week 02 — Human Capabilities and the Laws]]
1. Perception — what the eye does before you think
2. Gestalt — grouping is a design decision, not an accident
3. Working memory, chunking and cognitive load
4. Fitts's Law, Hick's Law and the real cost of a click
5. Human error — slips vs mistakes, forcing functions, undo over confirm
6. **Studio A** — cognitive audit of your target flow

### [[Week 03 — Interaction, State and Evaluation]]
1. Nielsen's 10 heuristics, properly understood
2. Modes, state and visibility — the most common structural bug
3. Feedback and the response-time budget
4. Heuristic evaluation as a method
5. Cognitive walkthrough — the four questions
6. **Studio A** — full evaluation writeup

---

## PHASE 2 · THE PRODUCT LAYER — *deciding what to build*
> The phase that separates a UI designer from a product designer. You stop receiving problems and start framing them.

### [[Week 04 — Problem Framing and Product Sense]]
1. Problem, symptom, solution — telling them apart
2. Jobs to be Done, and its honest limits
3. Point of View statements and How Might We
4. Appetite over estimates — Shape Up's core move
5. Product sense — building the intuition deliberately
6. **Studio A close** + **Studio B kickoff**

### [[Week 05 — Research That Changes Decisions]]
1. Needfinding — and what people cannot tell you
2. Running an interview without leading the witness
3. Synthesis — transcripts to insight without hallucinating
4. Personas: why they mostly fail, and what to use instead
5. Usability testing — the cheapest high-value thing you will ever do
6. **Studio B** — run three real interviews

### [[Week 06 — Flows, Structure and Information Architecture]]
1. Task analysis — decomposing a goal into steps
2. Flow diagrams — the artifact that makes you a product designer
3. Information architecture, navigation, findability
4. The state matrix — every screen is really *n* screens
5. Progressive disclosure and Tesler's Law
6. **Studio B** — full flow + state map

---

## PHASE 3 · THE CRAFT LAYER — *making it real*
> Your home turf, re-pointed at product. Compressed on the things you know, aggressive on the things that transfer badly from brand and motion work.

### [[Week 07 — Interface Craft and the Grid Beneath]]
1. The visual designer's trap — why product UI isn't a poster
2. Spacing systems and the 8pt grid as a language
3. Typographic scale for interfaces, not for brand
4. Colour as semantics — status, hierarchy, tokens
5. Layout under pressure — real data, long strings, resizing
6. **Studio B** — screens from your flows

### [[Week 08 — Design Systems and Component Thinking]]
1. What a design system actually is — the three layers
2. Tokens — the abstraction that makes design scale
3. Component API thinking — props, variants, slots, composition
4. Reading the great systems: Material, HIG, Polaris, Carbon
5. Governance, contribution and the politics of a system
6. **Studio B close** — build the mini-system behind your screens

### [[Week 09 — Motion, Latency and Time]]
1. Motion's four jobs in product — and the one job it doesn't have
2. Duration, easing and the physics of trust
3. Choreography, shared elements, spatial continuity
4. The response-time budget and the Doherty Threshold
5. Designing for latency — skeletons, optimism, perceived performance
6. **Studio C kickoff** — the AI feature brief

---

## PHASE 4 · THE PROFESSIONAL LAYER — *shipping, and being trusted*
> Everything that determines whether your work survives contact with a real company.

### [[Week 10 — Accessibility and the Unhappy Path]]
1. Accessibility as a design constraint, not a compliance task
2. WCAG in practice — contrast, focus, target size, semantics
3. Keyboard, screen readers and the invisible interface
4. Empty, loading, error, partial, offline — the five states you forget
5. Content design — writing as an interface material
6. **Studio C** — state and edge-case matrix

### [[Week 11 — Designing AI Products]]
1. What changes when the system is probabilistic
2. Microsoft's 18 Human–AI guidelines
3. Trust calibration — confidence, explanation, graceful wrongness
4. The interaction primitives of AI products
5. Evaluation and feedback loops as UX surface
6. **Studio C** — design the AI interaction

### [[Week 12 — Measurement, Critique and Shipping]]
1. HEART and Goals–Signals–Metrics
2. Experiments — what A/B testing can and cannot tell you
3. Critique — how to run one, how to survive one
4. Design rationale — the doc that gets you trusted
5. Working with engineering and PM — handoff, scope, appetite
6. **The case study** — packaging Studio C as a portfolio piece

---

## Load balance

| Phase | Weeks | Emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| Foundations | 1–3 | Heavy reading, light making. This is the base you said you lack. |
| Product layer | 4–6 | Heavy thinking and talking to humans. Least comfortable phase. |
| Craft layer | 7–9 | Heavy making. Fastest weeks for you. |
| Pro layer | 10–12 | Mixed. Week 11 is the one that pays your rent. |

If you must cut, cut **Week 07 to three days** — you can already do most of it. Never cut Weeks 4–6.
