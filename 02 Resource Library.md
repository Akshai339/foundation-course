## 2. Papers — the primary sources

You do not need to read all of these. The course points you at specific ones on specific days. Titles given so you can find them via Google Scholar or your library.

### Foundational theory
- ★ **Norman, D. (1986). "Cognitive Engineering."** In *User-Centered System Design*. — Where the Gulfs of Execution and Evaluation come from. The most load-bearing 30 pages in the field.
- ★ **Hutchins, Hollan & Norman (1985). "Direct Manipulation Interfaces."** *Human-Computer Interaction* 1(4). — Why dragging a file feels different from typing a move command. Still the best explanation of "feel."
- **Card, Moran & Newell (1983). *The Psychology of Human-Computer Interaction*.** — The book that made HCI a science. Introduced GOMS and the Model Human Processor. Chapter 2 is the one to read.
- **Licklider, J.C.R. (1960). "Man–Computer Symbiosis."** — Prophetic. Read it now that you work at an AI company; it's uncanny.
- **Weiser, M. (1991/1999). "The Computer for the 21st Century."** — Ubiquitous computing. The origin of "the best technology is invisible."

### Design principles & evaluation
- ★ **Nielsen & Molich (1990). "Heuristic evaluation of user interfaces."** *CHI '90*. — The method, from the source. Everyone quotes the ten heuristics; almost nobody reads the paper on how to actually run the evaluation.
- **Polson, Lewis, Rieman & Wharton (1992). "Cognitive walkthroughs: a method for theory-based evaluation of user interfaces."** — The four-question technique in Week 3.
- **Grudin, J. (1989). "The Case Against User Interface Consistency."** *CACM*. — A genuinely useful heresy. Consistency is a means, not a virtue.
- **Tognazzini, B. "First Principles of Interaction Design."** — Free at [asktog.com](https://asktog.com/atc/principles-of-interaction-design/). Longer and more opinionated than Nielsen's ten.
- **Gould, Boies, Levy, Richards & Schoonard (1987). "The 1984 Olympic Message System: a test of behavioral principles of system design."** *CACM*. — The original case for iterative user-centred design, and still the most persuasive one.

### Prototyping & process
- ★ **Houde, S. & Hill, C. (1997). "What do prototypes prototype?"** — Reframes prototyping into role / look-and-feel / implementation. Changes how you decide what to build. Short.
- **Rettig, M. (1994). "Prototyping for Tiny Fingers."** *CACM*. — The paper-prototyping classic. Absurdly practical.
- **Beaudouin-Lafon, M. & Mackay, W. (2003). "Prototyping tools and techniques."** — The comprehensive survey.

### Measurement
- ★ **Rodden, Hutchinson & Fu (2010). "Measuring the User Experience on a Large Scale: User-Centered Metrics for Web Applications."** *CHI 2010*. — **[Free PDF](https://research.google.com/pubs/archive/36299.pdf)**. The HEART framework. Also see [Kerry Rodden's own summary](https://kerryrodden.com/heart/).
- **John, B. & Kieras, D. (1996). "The GOMS family of user interface analysis techniques."** — How to predict task time without users. Niche but powerful.

### Cognition in the world
- **Hutchins, E. (1995). "How a cockpit remembers its speeds."** *Cognitive Science*. — Distributed cognition. Will permanently change how you think about interfaces as memory.
- **Mander, Salomon & Wong (1992). "A 'pile' metaphor for supporting casual organization of information."** — Beautiful example of designing from observed behaviour rather than logic.

### Ethics & consequence
- **Winner, L. (1980). "Do Artifacts Have Politics?"** *Daedalus*. — Not optional if you're building AI products.
- **Friedman, Kahn, Borning & Huldtgren (2013). "Value Sensitive Design and Information Systems."**

### AI-specific
- ★ **Amershi et al. (2019). "Guidelines for Human-AI Interaction."** *CHI 2019*, Microsoft Research. — The 18 guidelines. The nearest thing the field has to a standard. Companion toolkit below.

## 3. Books

### Buy these four (the core)
1. ★ **Don Norman — *The Design of Everyday Things* (revised ed.)** — The base of the base. Chapters 1, 2 and 5 are on the syllabus.
2. ★ **Jeff Johnson — *Designing with the Mind in Mind* (3rd ed.)** — Cognitive psychology aimed directly at UI decisions. The most practically useful book on this list for someone with your background.
3. ★ **Jon Yablonski — *Laws of UX* (2nd ed.)** — Short. The free companion site is [lawsofux.com](https://lawsofux.com/) and is genuinely most of the value.
4. ★ **Adam Wathan & Steve Schoger — *Refactoring UI*** — The one visual-craft book that's actually about product UI rather than posters. You'll move fast through it, and it will still fix things.

### Worth owning eventually
- **Jenifer Tidwell et al. — *Designing Interfaces*** — The pattern encyclopedia. A reference, not a read-through.
- **Alan Cooper — *About Face***, and **_The Inmates Are Running the Asylum_** — Cooper is abrasive and often right. Goal-directed design.
- **Erika Hall — *Just Enough Research*** — The antidote to research theatre. Short, sharp, funny.
- **Steve Portigal — *Interviewing Users*** — The best book on the actual craft of the interview.
- **Rogers, Sharp & Preece — *Interaction Design: Beyond Human-Computer Interaction*** — The standard textbook. Dry but complete.
- **Goodman, Kuniavsky & Moed — *Observing the User Experience*** — The research methods bible.
- **Jim Kalbach — *Mapping Experiences*** — Journey maps, service blueprints, ecosystem maps, done properly.
- **Torrey Podmajersky — *Strategic Writing for UX*** — Content design. Undervalued and high leverage.
- **Steve Krug — *Don't Make Me Think*** — Dated examples, immortal principles. Read in one sitting.
- **Kevin Mullet & Darrell Sano — *Designing Visual Interfaces* (1995)** — Out of print, findable. The most rigorous treatment of visual structure in UI ever written.
- **Christensen et al. — *Competing Against Luck*** — Jobs to be Done from the source.
- **Luke Wroblewski — *Web Form Design*** — Forms are 60% of product design. This is the only book on them.

### Free books
- ★ **[Shape Up — Ryan Singer / Basecamp](https://basecamp.com/shapeup)** — Free, online, complete. Shaping, appetite, betting, circuit breakers. The best free book on how product work is actually scoped.
- **[The Design of Everyday Things — Udacity course version](https://www.udacity.com/course/design-of-everyday-things--design101)** — Norman teaching his own book, free.

## 4. Practitioner writing — the working canon

- ★ **[Nielsen Norman Group articles](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/)** — 30 years of usability research, free. Enormous. The course points you at specific pieces; don't try to browse it.
- ★ **[Laws of UX](https://lawsofux.com/)** — 30 principles, each with definition, origins and takeaways. Your Week 2 spine.
- **[Julie Zhuo — The Year of the Looking Glass](https://medium.com/the-year-of-the-looking-glass)** — Ex-VP Design at Facebook. Start with ["What Designers Can Learn from PMs"](https://medium.com/the-year-of-the-looking-glass/what-designers-can-learn-from-pms-3aa285f511bc).
- **[Jules Walter — How to develop product sense](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/product-sense)** (Lenny's Newsletter) — The most concrete method anyone has published for building product judgment deliberately.
- **[How we do design critiques at Figma](https://www.figma.com/blog/design-critiques-at-figma/)** — and the longer [Noah Levin version](https://nlevin.medium.com/design-critiques-at-figma-799d4a3a1b0). Six critique formats. Week 12.
- **[UX Collective](https://uxdesign.cc/)** — High variance, occasionally excellent. Good for staying current.
- **[Inclusive Components — Heydon Pickering](https://inclusive-components.design/)** — Accessible component patterns, deeply reasoned. Week 10.
- **[Growth.Design case studies](https://growth.design/case-studies)** — Psychology-annotated teardowns of real products. Fast, visual, high signal.

## 5. Design systems — read these like literature

These are the best-documented design thinking on the public internet. Week 8 uses all four.

| System | Read it for |
|---|---|
| ★ **[Material Design 3](https://m3.material.io/)** | The most complete public system. Token architecture, adaptive layout, and the best public writing on motion. |
| ★ **[Apple Human Interface Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/)** | Platform philosophy. Less "here's a component," more "here's what we believe." The *writing* is the lesson. |
| ★ **[Shopify Polaris](https://polaris.shopify.com/)** | Best-in-class **content** and usage guidance. When to use a thing, and — rarer — when not to. |
| ★ **[IBM Carbon](https://carbondesignsystem.com/)** | Enterprise density, the 2x grid, best-in-class [motion](https://carbondesignsystem.com/elements/motion/overview/) and data-viz guidance. |
| [Atlassian Design System](https://atlassian.design/) | Excellent content design and pattern rationale. |
| [Adobe Spectrum](https://spectrum.adobe.com/) | Rigorous token and scaling model. |
| [GitLab Pajamas](https://design.gitlab.com/) | Radically transparent — you can watch the governance happen in public. |
| [Uber Base](https://base.uber.com/) | Strong theming architecture. |
| [Material 1 motion: duration & easing](https://m1.material.io/motion/duration-easing.html) | Still the clearest single page on motion timing in UI. Week 9. |

## 6. AI product design — the newest and least-settled area

- ★ **[Microsoft HAX Toolkit](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/haxtoolkit/)** — The [18 Guidelines for Human-AI Interaction](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/haxtoolkit/ai-guidelines/), a [design pattern library](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/haxtoolkit/design-patterns/), and a planning workbook. Organised by *when* in the interaction: initially, during, when wrong, over time.
- ★ **[Google PAIR — People + AI Guidebook](https://pair.withgoogle.com/guidebook-v2/)** — Pattern-based, question-driven. Strongest on mental models, explainability and feedback loops.
- **[Anthropic docs & cookbook](https://docs.claude.com/)** — Read as a designer, not an engineer: how a model's capabilities and failure modes constrain what the interface can promise.

## 7. Pattern and inspiration references

Use these as *evidence*, never as a shortcut past thinking.

- ★ **[Mobbin](https://mobbin.com/)** — Screen-by-screen flows from real shipped apps. The best "how did a serious team solve this" reference that exists. Some free, mostly £.
- **[Page Flows](https://pageflows.com/)** — Recorded user flows and journeys rather than static screens. £
- **[UI Patterns](https://ui-patterns.com/)** — Older, free, organised by problem rather than by app.
- **[Really Good UX](https://www.reallygoodux.io/)** — Curated flows with commentary.
- **[Nicely Done](https://nicelydone.club/)** — Web app UI reference.

## 10. Paid courses — only if you want them

You do not need any of these; the course above is complete without them. In honest order of value for *your* specific gap:

1. **[Interaction Design Specialization (UCSD, Coursera)](https://www.coursera.org/specializations/interaction-design)** — The closest thing to a real university sequence, and auditable free. If you buy one thing, buy this.
2. **NN/g UX Certification** — Expensive. Buy it only if you need the credential for a role change, not for the learning.
3. **Interaction Design Foundation (IxDF)** — Broad, cheap annual membership, variable depth. Good for breadth, weak for craft.
4. **Google UX Design Certificate (Coursera)** — Aimed well below your level. Skip.
